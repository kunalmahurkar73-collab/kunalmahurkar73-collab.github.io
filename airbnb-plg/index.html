EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The booking starts
before the search.

Group travel is a sequence of shared decisions. This proposal tests whether making constraints explicit helps groups reach a decision they can keep.

The customer problem

One organiser reconciles dates, different budgets and trust concerns across a group. Coordination and repeat planning are discovery hypotheses, supported by an illustrative dataset and awaiting genuine participant evidence.

The product bet

Start with a Group Decision Room for domestic leisure groups from Mumbai and Pune. Reuse opted-in group preferences for the next trip. Help hosts prepare for those needs, then offer optional local activities after booking.

The decision that matters

Advance only when completed stays improve without worsening cancellations, complaints or trust. A 10% relative conversion uplift is a proposed target. No experiment has run and no commercial impact is claimed.

PROPOSED NORTH STARMonthly completed domestic group stays

A distinct India domestic stay with ≥3 registered guests, group_id and stay_completed event in the calendar month. Exclude cancellations, staff and duplicate reservations. This metric cannot be measured from the public prototype.

PROBLEM DISCOVERY

Separate what we know
from what we need to learn.

Secondary evidence

Travel is social

Public research and current platform capabilities establish a relevant context. They cannot establish the conversion impact of this proposal. Source-level limitations appear in the reference register.

Synthetic assumption

Coordination is costly

The dataset deliberately assigns coordination friction to 68 of 100 fictional travellers. It demonstrates analysis, rather than estimating prevalence in India.

Research question

Which constraint blocks commitment?

Ask about the last actual trip before introducing concepts. Compare budget, date, property reliability and price concerns. Observe a real planning task before deciding what to build.

RESEARCH INSIGHTS

Explore the evidence.
Know its limits.

Twenty source-linked public observations inform the product questions. A separate illustrative dataset demonstrates analytical methods.

Method and evidence limits

Public sources were selected purposively across travel, accommodation, hosting and adjacent commute discussions. Known cross-posts were excluded; same-author replies were consolidated. Identities and reported experiences were not independently verified. Theme counts are not population estimates. The illustrative dataset remains separate.

PERSONAS & JOBS TO BE DONE

Design for the organiser.
Respect every traveller.

Provisional personas derived from synthetic scenarios. They are fictional design tools.

Fictional · traveller

The reluctant organiser

A working adult in Mumbai coordinating four friends. Wants a decision without becoming everyone's travel agent.

When friends have different constraints, help us find a feasible stay so I can book with confidence.
Validate: time spent reconciling budgets, not just wishlist usage.
Fictional · family traveller

The cautious planner

A Pune resident organising a multigenerational break. Accessibility and clear total cost matter more than a large option set.

When travelling with family, help me check suitability so no one faces a surprise on arrival.
Validate: which claims need host confirmation.
Fictional · host

The independent host

A small-property operator deciding where to invest. Needs concrete group-readiness advice without promises of demand.

When improving my listing, show me relevant needs so I can prioritise affordable changes.
Validate: operational cost and willingness to act.

COMPETITOR ANALYSIS

Build beyond what
already exists.

Existing Airbnb shared wishlists, group messaging, comparison, itineraries and Experiences are a capability baseline. This concept explores explicit individual constraints, feasibility and consent-based reuse. It does not claim to invent collaborative travel.
