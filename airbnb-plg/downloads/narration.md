# Airbnb India: five-minute executive presentation

Kunal Mahurkar · Independent AI-First Product Management course assignment

## 0:00–0:50 · Slide 1 · Domestic group travel

“I’m Kunal Mahurkar. This independent case study explores how Airbnb could make domestic group travel easier in India and earn more repeat stays. The proposed North Star is monthly completed domestic group stays. The first decision is whether a structured group decision workflow solves a problem that existing shared wishlists and messaging do not already solve. The website contains four working simulation prototypes. No booking, invitation or payment is real.”

## 0:50–1:45 · Slide 2 · Evidence and uncertainty

“I separated public secondary sources from an illustrative research dataset. There are one hundred synthetic travellers, twenty synthetic hosts and twelve fictional interview scenarios. Zero genuine responses or completed interviews have been verified for this release. The synthetic dataset intentionally encodes sixty-eight percent coordination friction and sixty-one percent trust concerns. These are assumptions supplied for demonstrating analysis, not customer findings. Dashboard filters calculate their results from those records. The real-research view stays empty until anonymized, reviewed evidence becomes available. Research will test whether coordination deserves priority.”

## 1:45–2:45 · Slide 3 · Four product experiments

“The Group Decision Room adds explicit individual budgets, availability and conflict visibility to existing collaboration capabilities. The Repeat Travel Engine reuses a saved group’s preferences through transparent rules. The Host Growth Assistant turns aggregated requirements into an explainable readiness checklist. Local Experience Bundles let a group optionally add activities after a stay decision. Airbnb already offers Experiences. The proposal is a contextual, optional discovery and itinerary workflow, not the invention of experiences. Every demo has a clear scope, a success measure and guardrails. Simulated completion provides interface feedback only.”

## 2:45–3:55 · Slide 4 · Economics and pilot

“The financial model is entirely illustrative. Forty thousand baseline bookings, ten percent uplift and twelve thousand rupees per booking produce four thousand incremental bookings and four point eight crore rupees incremental gross booking value. At a modeled fifteen point five percent effective fee, accommodation fee revenue is seventy-four point four lakh. Adding sixteen lakh experience revenue gives ninety point four lakh before incremental costs. Gross booking value is not revenue. Contribution and ROI depend on the costs you enter. A ninety-day Mumbai and Pune pilot would randomize groups to control or treatment. The ten percent relative conversion lift is a target, not a result. A three-percentage-point repeat-booking target needs one-hundred-eighty-day follow-up beyond the pilot.”

## 3:55–5:00 · Slide 5 · Decision and next evidence

“I recommend a discovery gate before major engineering investment. Twenty real responses support early discovery, fifty help surface patterns, and one hundred trigger a structured priority review. Five to ten usability tests examine whether people can reach a shared decision unaided. At least ten host interviews reassess the supply strategy. These are learning milestones, not statistical significance guarantees. Product owns the decision, analytics verifies instrumentation, engineering owns reliability, and trust, legal and host operations review the rollout. Cancellation, complaints, host satisfaction and privacy act as guardrails. This independent product management case study currently uses publicly available secondary research and a clearly labelled synthetic customer dataset to demonstrate research analysis, hypothesis formation, feature prioritization and MVP design. Primary customer research is ongoing. Synthetic responses are not actual participant responses and are excluded from verified research findings. Product recommendations will be reassessed as genuine customer and host feedback becomes available.”

Read at approximately 125 words per minute. Rehearse the sequence and adjust pauses to finish within five minutes. Do not suggest that studies or experiments have already run.
